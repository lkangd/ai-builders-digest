2026-10-21 12:55
Pretty cool that we clearly have an emerging form factor for what AI might look like for the foreseeable future as autonomous systems dedicated to specific tasks or domains.

This is a huge leap past the LLM wrapper days because the new expectation is that you can finally farm out more sophisticated work to the agent to tackle for you. And you can reliably trust that work can get accomplished properly (still with a human in the loop) because of far better tool and computer use, and the general intelligence of the models improving.

1) The tool and computer use capability increases in the models mean that you can provide the agent with far more granular access to external data systems and other software to operate. This means, in effect, the agent begins to execute workflows similar to how a person would have in that same function. And where there isn’t existing software, the agent just builds it itself. This is a huge unlock.

2) Next, the actual capability of the models are improving in just about every vertical domain. The models can make far better judgment calls in legal, do deeper bio research, scan for security issues in code, understand supply chain processes, and more. Obviously in many of these spaces then models still have worse intuition than people, but that will only get better.

3) Finally, the harnesses and surrounding infra — sandboxes, file systems, data pipelines, etc. — allows for longer running agents, agents to be able to work on arbitrarily complex or long tasks, process data at enormous scale, and more. Few of these components worked well for AI even a year ago, but we now have many of the core primitives necessary for more autonomous systems.

When combined with the right UX, implementation, and change management for the human oversight and coordination, it’s clear that these autonomous systems will become quite powerful in a wide variety of enterprise workflows.

We’re starting to see them in IT automation, cyber operations, drug discovery, manufacturing, AI research, and more spaces. You can almost imagine one or more of these systems for every role in the economy. Quite a bit of opportunity ahead.
译文
一种新兴形态正逐渐清晰：在可预见的未来，AI 可能长成专注于特定任务或领域的自主系统，这相当酷。

这远远超越了 LLM wrapper 时代，因为新的预期是：你终于可以把更复杂的工作交给智能体处理，并且能可靠地相信工作会被妥善完成（仍需人类在环），这得益于工具和计算机使用能力大幅提升，以及模型通用智能不断进步。

1）模型中工具和计算机使用能力的提升，意味着你可以让智能体以更细粒度访问外部数据系统和其他软件来操作。实际上，智能体会开始执行与同一职能中人类相似的工作流。没有现成软件时，智能体就自己构建。这是巨大的突破。

2）其次，模型的实际能力几乎在每个垂直领域都在提升。模型能在法律领域做出更好的判断，开展更深入的生物研究，扫描代码安全问题，理解供应链流程等。显然，在许多这类领域，模型的直觉仍不如人，但这只会越来越好。

3）最后，运行框架和周边基础设施——沙箱、文件系统、数据管道等——支持更长时间运行的智能体，让智能体能处理任意复杂或长时任务、以巨大规模处理数据等。一年前，这些组件中很少有能很好地用于 AI 的，但我们现在已具备更多自主系统所需的许多核心原语。

再结合合适的用户体验、实施方式，以及围绕人类监督与协调的变革管理，显然这些自主系统将在广泛的企业工作流中变得相当强大。

我们开始看到它们出现在 IT 自动化、网络运营、药物发现、制造、AI 研究等空间。你几乎可以想象，经济中的每个角色都可能对应一个或多个这类系统。未来机会很多。

BOOK REVIEW: Will and Attention, Meghan O'Gieblyn author of God, Human, Animal Machine

I loved this book. O'Gieblyn writes with an addictive mix of clarity, rigor, literary references, and self-implicating honesty. I tore through it in a few days.

The book deals with her relapse into alcoholism, how the stories we tell about ourselves can both save us and, when they go stale, ruin us, whether we change from the outside in or the inside out, and how and whether we can trust our own desires.

Underneath it's a story about pragmatism. Her initial recovery from alcoholism rested on a pragmatic philosophy: she treated the core tenets of sobriety—faith as "something you do" surrendering her will to a Higher Power that would keep her safe, and even the resolution not to drink—not as capital T truths, but truths she adopted because they worked.

The problem she found is, for one, this creates a gap between your underlying desires and beliefs and your behavior. One that you hope will close eventually, but if it doesn’t can leave you feeling hollowed out and can keep your actual desires and beliefs unexamined. The other problem, of course, is once you start asking whether sobriety is “useful”, you open yourself to another version of the truth: That it might not be.

One of the things I found so striking about this book is how honest she is, without glorifying drinking, about what alcohol gives to alcoholics. For her, it can bring the kind of unification of self that mystics and yogis seek in God and meditation. So at that point, it is easy for the alcoholic to ask, fairly, why alcohol is not “useful”?

It should be clear that even if you’re not an alcoholic these are the kinds of mental gymnastics all of us do to justify and rationalize any number of behaviors or beliefs.

As an admirer of pragmatism I found this to be quite an interesting and mind-expanding line of questioning.

But I also found it interesting that, as far as I can tell, her turn away from pragmatism—away from truths held to because they “worked,” and back toward a vision of truth and goodness as inviolable and untouchable by experience—ultimately seems to rest on pragmatic grounds. Getting sober again, having faith, and treating the resolution not to drink as an unconditional taboo all, in the broadest sense possible, rest on what they do for her: they keep her desires from talking her back into ruin.

书评：Will and Attention，God, Human, Animal Machine 作者 Meghan O'Gieblyn

我很喜欢这本书。O'Gieblyn 的写作令人上瘾地融合了清晰、严谨、文学引用和把自己也摆进去的坦诚。我几天就读完了。

这本书讲她酗酒复发，讲我们讲述的关于自己的故事如何既能拯救我们、又会在陈旧后毁掉我们，讲我们是从外向内还是从内向外改变，以及我们能否、如何信任自己的欲望。

底层是一个关于实用主义的故事。她最初戒酒靠的是一种实用主义哲学：她把戒酒的核心信条——信仰是“你去做的事”、将意志交给能护她安全的 Higher Power，甚至不喝酒的决心——不当作大写 T 的真理，而是当作因为有效才采纳的真理。

她发现的问题之一是，这会在你底层的欲望、信念与行为之间制造鸿沟。你希望它最终弥合，但若不能，会让你感到被掏空，也会让你的真实欲望和信念得不到审视。另一个问题当然是，一旦你开始问戒酒是否“有用”，你就向另一种真相敞开：它可能没用。

这本书最打动我的一点是，她非常诚实地写出酒精给了酗酒者什么，又不美化饮酒。对她来说，酒精能带来神秘主义者和瑜伽士在 God 与冥想中寻求的那种自我统一。于是到那一步，酗酒者很容易也有理由问：为什么酒精不“有用”？

应当清楚，即使你不是酗酒者，我们也都会做这类心理体操，为各种行为和信念找理由、合理化。

作为实用主义的赞赏者，我觉得这条追问非常有趣、开阔思路。

但我也觉得有趣：据我所见，她背离实用主义——不再因为“有效”而坚持真理，转而回到一种真理与善不可侵犯、经验无法触及的图景——最终似乎仍建立在实用主义理由上。再次戒酒、拥有信仰、把不喝酒的决心当作无条件禁忌，从最广义上说，都取决于它们为她做了什么：它们阻止她的欲望把她劝回毁灭。
