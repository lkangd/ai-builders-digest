This post nails why we why we should take the Riemann Hypothesis breakthrough very seriously, even beyond math. We’re entering an era where solving problems will look very different from how we solved them in the past.

“This is not theory-building in the usual sense. It is something much weirder and more alien. The best human mathematicians know how to build theory, painstakingly, to reach a goal. But no human brain, on its own, is capable of creating a wormhole that requires a 25,000 page long tour through existing infrastructure with no obvious or realistic connection to the goal. But huge credit to Levent here: I think no current AI, on its own, is capable of this either.”

It used to be the case that to solve a particular problem - code, life sciences, math, cyber, engineering, and so on - you were ultimately bound by how much information an individual or team could retain and understand about the space. More information or more team members don’t always help, because it gets harder to connect the dots or coordinate amongst everyone. AI changes that completely.

For the first time ever we can decouple the human context limits and ability to work through information from our ability to drive productivity or discoveries. This is likely not just a small increase in the kinds of discoveries we can drive, but a qualitative shift in human output.

Now every single form of prior art, insight, past research, past decision, or unrelated breakthrough, can be used for the discovery or creation of something new. This is useful for anything from cybersecurity to life sciences research. Agents will be able to correlate entirely unrelated work to a separate project; scan every bit of data in your enterprise for solving a problem; and so on.

The solutions may not be as elegant as they were in the past, but we sure will have far more of them. We’re only in the first couple of months of actually understanding the implications of this but it’s clearly going to drive a lot of change.
译文
这篇帖子说得很准，为什么我们即使在数学之外，也应认真对待 Riemann Hypothesis 的突破。我们正在进入这样一个时代：解决问题的方式，将与过去截然不同。

“这不是通常意义上的理论构建。它要诡异得多，也更异质。最优秀的人类数学家知道如何辛苦地搭建理论，以达到一个目标。但没有任何一个人类大脑，单凭自己，能够造出这样一条虫洞：它需要长达 25,000 页的既有基础设施之旅，而且与目标之间没有明显或现实的关联。不过这里要大大归功于 Levent：我认为目前也没有任何 AI 能独自做到这一点。”

过去，要解决一个特定问题，无论是代码、生命科学、数学、网络安全、工程还是其他领域，最终都会受限于个人或团队能够记住并理解该领域多少信息。更多信息或更多团队成员并不总是有帮助，因为把点连起来、或在所有人之间协调，会变得更难。AI 彻底改变了这一点。

我们第一次可以把人类的上下文限制、以及消化信息的能力，与推动生产力或发现的能力解耦。这很可能不只是我们发现种类的小幅增加，而是人类产出的一次质变。

现在，每一种先前成果、洞见、既往研究、过去的决策，或看似无关的突破，都可以被用来发现或创造出新的东西。从网络安全到生命科学研究，这都有用。Agent 将能把完全不相关的工作与另一个项目关联起来；扫描企业里的每一份数据来解决问题；诸如此类。

解决方案也许不如过去那么优雅，但我们一定会拥有多得多的方案。我们才刚刚用头几个月去理解这件事的含义，但它显然将带来大量变化。

Abhishek Saha@ObhishekSaha
Like most mathematicians, I believed the Riemann Hypothesis to be completely out of reach at present (even with the best AI tools), because I expected it to require substantial theory-building, probably in arithmetic geometry. The kind of new theory-building that fills entire books, and that AI isn't so good at yet.

Mathematicians have a notion of how deep a problem is. A sufficiently deep problem is generally intractable until enough of the theory is in place. If difficult problems are like valuable mineral reserves buried miles underground, you first need to build the infrastructure to reach them.

What Levent's proof seems to have done instead is something completely different. It takes an incredibly complicated tour through the infrastructure built for a hundred other mineral reserves, situated far from the destination, and somehow create a wormhole to the original destination.

This is not theory-building in the usual sense. It is something much weirder and more alien. The best human mathematicians know how to build theory, painstakingly, to reach a goal. But no human brain, on its own, is capable of creating a wormhole that requires a 25,000 page long tour through existing infrastructure with no obvious or realistic connection to the goal. But huge credit to Levent here: I think no current AI, on its own, is capable of this either.

I had never worked on or thought too much about the Riemann Hypothesis: it seemed very far removed from my own area of number theory.

But the astonishing thing about Levent's claimed proof of RH (which I think has a very good chance of being correct) is that it uses a huge number of precisely the things that are central to my own corner of number theory:

- Theta functions and other automorphic forms on unitary groups
- Gan–Gross–Prasad period formulas
- Relative trace formulas

Etc., etc.

A problem that I had always assumed would require new theories in arithmetic geometry may instead turn out to be tractable through an inhuman chain of existing (deep) results in higher-rank automorphic forms (and a lot else).
