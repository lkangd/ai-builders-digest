2026-11-11 10:30
I look at what I do with Fable 5.2 in the cracks of my week and think: this would've taken five people and $200,000.

Then I look at what my team does with the same AI.

Last week, between meetings, I analyzed our customer survey, made a game for our team, spotted an opportunity to sell @Every to teams, built a CRM, and started reaching out.

Look at that and you might ask: Why would I need a team at all?

The chart seems to agree. On OpenAI's GDPval benchmark, AI went from beating human experts 12.4% of the time in 2024 to around 83% in 2026. That's the aggregate view of the future of work: gather a lot of data and extrapolate a trend line.

But the aggregate misses something. To see it, you have to get intimate: zoom in on one real situation, with all its messiness, and extrapolate from there.

We gave everyone at Every access to the same customer survey and the same AI models.

• I found an opportunity to sell to teams.
• @poojary_yash saw that first-time subscribers were churning because we send too much email.
• @dabrundage noticed that many of our readers feel lonely: they're excited about AI, but people around them think they're crazy.
• @jackcheng turned the results into a Claymation game show.

Could you ask Claude to produce what each of them found? Maybe. But generating possibilities is cheap. Knowing which ones matter is hard.

@hammer_mt ran an experiment to predict what I'd say in Slack. When Claude got 400 attempts, it produced an answer like mine 93% of the time. But when it had to rank its own answers, mine landed in its top 10% only around 60% of the time.

Each person on a team is responsible for a different part of the world. That responsibility narrows where they look, and their perspective helps them decide what matters enough to act on. A team explores more possibilities than a single default prompt, and it can take responsibility for what it finds.

The obvious objection: models will get better at ranking. I think they will. But people aren't frozen while that happens. Comparing 2026 Yash with 2027 Claude misses that there will also be a 2027 Yash, with another year of experience, using 2027 Claude.

As models improve and spread, they raise the floor for everyone. The default answer becomes the baseline. The value is in using your perspective and responsibility to push past it.

That's why I think we need to change how we measure AI. Instead of only asking how well a model performs on problems everyone agrees are worth solving, ask: How good is it at doing my work, from my perspective?

The aggregate can show us the capability curve. The intimate helps us see what we might build, and who we might become, as we ride it.

I presented this at Thesis: 2027. The full talk is on the Every podcast:
译文
我利用一周里的零碎时间用 Fable 5.2 做了一些事，然后想：这本来要五个人和 20 万美元。

然后我看看团队用同一套 AI 做的东西。

上周，在会议间隙，我分析了客户调查，给团队做了个游戏，发现了把 @Every 卖给团队的机会，搭了一个 CRM，并开始联系客户。

看到这些，你可能会问：那我为什么还需要团队？

图表似乎也同意。在 OpenAI 的 GDPval 基准上，AI 击败人类专家的比例从 2024 年的 12.4% 升到 2026 年的约 83%。这是未来工作的总体视角：收集大量数据，外推趋势线。

但总体视角漏掉了一些东西。要看到它，你得贴近看：放大一个真实场景，连同它的所有混乱，再从那里外推。

我们让 Every 每个人都拿到同一份客户调查和同样的 AI 模型。

• 我发现了卖给团队的机会。
• @poojary_yash 看到首次订阅者流失，是因为我们发的邮件太多。
• @dabrundage 注意到很多读者感到孤独：他们对 AI 很兴奋，但身边的人觉得他们疯了。
• @jackcheng 把结果做成了黏土动画游戏节目。

你能让 Claude 产出他们各自发现的东西吗？也许可以。但生成可能性很便宜，知道哪些重要很难。

@hammer_mt 做了一个实验，预测我会在 Slack 里说什么。给 Claude 400 次尝试时，它有 93% 的概率给出像我的答案。但当它要给自己的答案排序时，我的答案只有约 60% 的时候进入它的前 10%。

团队里的每个人负责世界的不同部分。这种责任缩小了他们关注的范围，而他们的视角帮他们判断什么重要到值得行动。团队比单个默认提示词探索更多可能性，也能为发现的东西负责。

显而易见的反对意见：模型会越来越会排序。我认为会。但人不会在这一切发生时冻住。拿 2026 年的 Yash 和 2027 年的 Claude 比，忽略了还会有 2027 年的 Yash，带着又多一年的经验，用着 2027 年的 Claude。

随着模型进步和普及，它们会抬高所有人的下限。默认答案成为基线。价值在于用你的视角和责任去越过它。

这就是为什么我认为需要改变衡量 AI 的方式。不要只问模型在大家都认为值得解决的问题上表现多好，而要问：从我的视角看，它做我的工作有多好？

There are basically two kinds of companies to build right now.

1. Diffusion of AI
This is the "vertical SaaS" of today. There are a bunch of different models for it (FDE company, rollup, horizontal product) but ultimately it all boils down to the same thing: get AI diffused very close to where the work is being done.

Profound, Cognition, Harvey etc. A lot of great companies will get built here.

2. Frontier of AI
This is where the really interesting action is. Use AI to accelerate scientific progress, then build products from that progress.

It is a harder category to see. But we are seeing a lot more of it at @spc:

- Externa is adding hundreds of new chemical building blocks to proteins, so you can engineer a drug to match the disease instead of settling for what nature gave you.

- Epana Bio is using genomics and AI to design autoimmune therapies that go after the cellular origin of the disease.

- VenomicsAI is doing AI-accelerated drug discovery from venoms.

- Equivariant is doing multi-physics modeling for the semiconductor industry.

(There are always picks-and-shovels for AI as well. Lots of amazing companies in that category.)

Category 2 is not well understood or much talked about externally. It should be because I think that is where the vast majority of model driven returns will come from.
