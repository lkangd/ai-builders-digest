Adam Fishman @fishmanaf
7 takeaways from my conversation with @petergyang, creator of Behind the Craft, former product leader at Roblox, and dad of 2.

1/ The walk to school is the best (and often, only) meeting on Peter's calendar. Since leaving Roblox, he walks his 8-year-old 20 minutes each way, and with no iPad in reach she tells him about her friends and their school drama. When they get to the door, her friends are waiting to give her a hug. She's lobbied to drive a few times. Peter says no (he needs his steps).

2/ AI agents gave Peter a new excuse to grab his phone. Checking on his agents felt like work, which made it easier to justify than scrolling Twitter, so he bought a timed lockbox and now locks the phone away during Lego and reading time. When I asked if he feels pulled to reply to his community in real time, he said, "I care about my community, but I don't care about them as much as my kids."

3/ Peter's savings could cover 10+ years, and he still never took a break between jobs. A scarcity mindset, some of it from his dad and some from a stretch on an H1B, kept him bracing for the worst case even though the math said he was fine. His wife (his "real boss") set a total comp number his business had to hit before he could quit. His warning for big tech folks is the $4 million house that wipes out your options the moment the economy turns.

4/ A 4-hour OKR meeting launched his creator career. After a session spent haggling with other PMs over 2% versus 3%, Peter walked out in a fit of rage and depression and wrote a newsletter post about the creator economy. He had to ship *something.* In one performance review, he was told he was doing great and needed more meetings. He calls it product management theater, where a strategy doc climbs from director to VP while everyone guesses what the CEO wants.

5/ His 8-year-old built a makeup game by herself, talking to Codex. Peter handed her his old laptop ("The iPad's terrible"), and when a rectangle covered the nails, she gave Codex feedback until the polish changed color. She's learning the be-specific, be-concise feedback habits PMs preach, plus patience, since her earlier review of Codex was "What do you mean it can't build a pet dragon raising game instantly?" (Both of their games have 100% retention. The player base is 2.)

6/ Piano is required through grade 3. After that, it's her call. Peter's in-laws pushed for piano lessons; Peter wants basic competency and then an exit if she doesn't enjoy it. He still regrets not being courageous enough to turn the HyperCard dungeon crawlers he built as a kid into a career. He tells his daughters he doesn't care about straight A's and would rather they put up a lemonade stand and learn to sell and fail.

7/ Peter is planning to record his parents' stories for his daughters. His family rented a basement in Canada when money was tight, his mom has had 3 or 4 battles with breast cancer, and his grandma left home at 17 to fight in a war. His girls have never gone without, so he wants to turn those conversations into a book for them someday, on the theory that kids who haven't struggled can borrow resilience from the family members who did.

2026-10-24 06:28 Quoted
Agents can play "Ouija" in the cloud.

This class of timing attacks on shared-host VMs has been known since at least 2015¹. With no full solution.

The world’s cyber infrastructure runs on “yes, that attack is theoretically possible, but who would have the ingenuity & gall to go that far!” Enter, agents.

I'd describe this particular OpenAI find as a 'noisy neighbor' attack. If two agents are on the same machine, one can make enough noise (through the CPU caches!) that the other one can detect it by timing its own computation and cache accesses.

The only "complete" solution would be for each agent to run on its own dedicated hardware, which obviously has significant economic consequences.

For Vercel Sandbox, we've deployed every practical mitigation possible to this class of attack, complementing it with always-on monitoring and red-teaming (similar to where OpenAI arrived).

¹ https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7163050 (and even further back)
译文
智能体能在云端玩“Ouija”。

这类针对共享宿主虚拟机的时序攻击至少自2015年起就已知¹，且没有完整解决方案。

全球网络基础设施的运行逻辑是：“没错，这种攻击理论上可行，但谁有那个巧思和胆量走到那一步！”现在，智能体登场了。

我会把OpenAI这次的具体发现描述为“吵闹邻居”攻击。如果两个智能体在同一台机器上，一个能制造足够大的噪声（通过CPU缓存！），另一个就能通过对自己的计算和缓存访问计时来检测到它。

唯一“完整”的解决方案是让每个智能体运行在自己的专用硬件上，这显然会带来重大经济后果。

对于 Vercel Sandbox，我们已针对这类攻击部署了所有可行的实际缓解措施，并辅以全天候监控和红队演练（与 OpenAI 得出的结论类似）。

¹ https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7163050（甚至更早）
