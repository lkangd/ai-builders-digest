augmenting human intellect

when i started making software, i wasn’t dreaming of a factory.

i loved that something on a screen could become an extension of my mind. a place to put a thought before i understood it. to move things around until new connections appeared. to make little systems that worked the way i worked, even if it made no sense to anyone else.

the computer wasn’t interesting because it could do work without me. it was interesting because of what i could do with it.

the early lineage of computing was built around this possibility. in 1962, Engelbart described augmenting human intellect as helping a person understand complex situations and find solutions that were previously out of reach. his starting point was the human being, not the autonomous machine.

he wasn’t describing a faster black box. he was looking at the whole relationship between a person, their tools, their language, their methods, and what they could learn to do with them. the capability belonged to that whole system.

somewhere along the way, we started telling a much smaller story about a much more powerful technology.

more automation. more agents. more software producing more software. more output from fewer people. more growth. more numbers to prove that something is happening.

and less attention to the person any of this is supposed to be for.

i don’t want to pretend computing had a pure innocent beginning. replacing human labor was one of its ambitions. even in 1960, Licklider distinguished human-computer symbiosis from systems where the remaining people mainly served the machine. he wanted computers to participate in the formation of ideas, not only execute problems already defined.

that distinction feels important – does the system help you discover what you mean? or does it only want you to submit a request, and wait outside while it works?

sometimes waiting is exactly what i want. please do the search. move the files. take away the repetitive work.

but not everything difficult is overhead.

sometimes moving the pieces around is how you understand them. sometimes making the thing changes what you wanted to make. sometimes you need to see the wrong version, touch it, take it apart, and discover a better question.

i want tools that leave room for that relationship.

in his 1981 paper on Smalltalk, Ingalls described a system meant to support everyone’s creative spirit. personal mastery mattered: an individual should be able to comprehend the system, rather than remain dependent on something permanently beyond their reach.

he also argued that computer models should fit the human mind, rather than demand the reverse.

not one perfect workflow for everyone. systems that different people can inhabit differently. someone who thinks through pictures. someone who needs to write. someone who understands a problem by building a simulation. someone whose way of organizing their life doesn’t resemble a SaaS dashboard.

a system you can reshape as your understanding changes.

this is what i want AI to make possible. not just pumping out software, but having a living relationship with it. being able to ask why something works this way. change one part without surrendering the rest. see what happened. undo it. learn enough to make the next change yourself.

the machine can become more capable without making the person smaller.

but that won’t happen just because we make the models better. we have to care about it. design for it. notice when we are making someone more dependent while calling it empowerment.

i don’t want to measure the future only by how much work happens without us.

i want to know what becomes possible for the person who couldn’t participate before.

what can they understand now? what can they express? what can they change without asking permission?

when i say augmenting human intellect, that is what i mean.

not a world that no longer needs us.
a world we have more ability to shape.

增强人类智能

我开始做软件时，梦想的不是一座工厂。

我喜欢屏幕上的东西能成为我思维的延伸。一个能在理解之前先安放想法的地方。一个能不断挪动事物，直到新联系浮现的地方。一个能做出按我的方式运转的小系统的地方，哪怕别人完全看不懂。

计算机的有趣之处，不在于它能脱离我工作，而在于我能用它做什么。

早期计算的发展脉络正是围绕这种可能性建立的。1962年，Engelbart 将增强人类智能描述为帮助人理解复杂情境，并找到此前无法触及的解决方案。他的起点是人，而不是自主机器。

他描述的不是一个更快的黑箱。他看到的是人、工具、语言、方法，以及人借助它们学会做什么之间的整体关系。能力属于这个整体系统。

后来不知从何时起，我们开始用一个狭隘得多的故事，来讲述一种强大得多的技术。

更多自动化。更多智能体。更多软件生产软件。更少的人产出更多输出。更多增长。更多数字来证明有事正在发生。

却更少关注这一切本该服务于谁。

我不想假装计算有一个纯洁无邪的开端。取代人力本就是它的野心之一。早在1960年，Licklider 就区分了人机共生与另一种系统：在那种系统里，剩下的人主要服务于机器。他希望计算机参与思想的形成，而不只是执行已经定义好的问题。

这个区分很重要——系统是帮你发现自己想表达什么，还是只要你提交请求，然后在外等候它工作？

有时等待正是我想要的。请去搜索。移动文件。替我处理重复性工作。

但并非所有困难都是额外负担。

有时挪动零件正是理解它们的方式。有时动手制作会改变你想做的东西。有时你需要看到错误版本，触摸它，拆开它，然后发现一个更好的问题。

我想要为这种关系留出空间的工具。

Ingalls 在1981年关于 Smalltalk 的论文中，描述了一个旨在支持每个人创造精神的系统。个人掌握很重要：一个人应当能理解系统，而不是永远依赖某种遥不可及的东西。

他还主张，计算机模型应适应人的心智，而不是要求人的心智去适应它。

不是给所有人一个完美工作流。而是不同的人能以不同方式栖居的系统。有人通过图像思考。有人需要写作。有人通过构建模拟来理解问题。有人组织生活的方式完全不像 SaaS 仪表盘。

一个能随着你的理解变化而重塑的系统。

这就是我希望 AI 使之成为可能的事。不只是批量生产软件，而是与软件保持一种鲜活的关系。能问为什么某件事这样运作。改变一部分而不用交出其余部分。看看发生了什么。撤销它。学到足够多，以便自己做出下一次改变。

机器可以变得更有能力，而不让人变得更渺小。

但这不会仅仅因为我们把模型做得更好就发生。我们必须在意它。为它设计。注意我们何时在让人更加依赖，却称之为赋能。

我不想只用有多少工作无需我们完成来衡量未来。

我想知道，对从前无法参与的人来说，什么变得可能。

他们现在能理解什么？能表达什么？能在不请求许可的情况下改变什么？

当我说增强人类智能时，我指的就是这个。

不是一个不再需要我们世界，而是一个我们更有能力塑造的世界。
